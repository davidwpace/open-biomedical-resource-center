# Contributing to the Open Biomedical Resource Center

Thank you for helping improve public understanding of biomedical science. **A useful contribution can be small.** You can verify a source, spot an error, reproduce a published example, or suggest a clear discovery story.

## Start here: choose one contribution

- **Check a public fact or report a research inconsistency:** [Submit a research observation](https://github.com/davidwpace/open-biomedical-resource-center/issues/new?template=research_observation.yml)
- **Suggest a scientifically grounded historical or recent breakthrough story:** [Propose a discovery story](https://github.com/davidwpace/open-biomedical-resource-center/issues/new?template=discovery_story.yml)
- **Suggest or correct a public resource listing:** [Open a resource suggestion](https://github.com/davidwpace/open-biomedical-resource-center/issues/new?template=resource_suggestion.md)
- **Improve source code or public documentation:** Fork the repository and send a pull request, ideally referencing an issue. No direct push to `main` is required.

A GitHub account is generally required to submit an Issue or pull request. Browsing and learning remain public and free.

## VERY IMPORTANT: this is a public repository, not a medical-case intake service

Do **not** put any of the following in GitHub Issues, Discussions, commits, pull requests, code snippets or attachments:

- private or de-identified patient cases, medical records, lab reports, imaging scans, genetic files, transcripts, or personal health details
- names, dates of care, locations, unusual event combinations, addresses, phone numbers, account details, private links or other direct/indirect identifiers
- other people's protected data, copyrighted full papers or licensed datasets without permission
- credentials, API keys, passwords, or unpublished confidential research

**Removing names or obvious PII is not enough:** indirect details can re-identify someone, particularly with rare conditions. GitHub histories and forks can retain published content even after deletion. We do not currently offer consent-based private case intake. Do not use these forms for it.

For supervised educational participation, do not include student personal information or health data. Follow any guardian, school or institution safeguards that apply.

## What makes a contribution valuable?

A clear, short note should answer four questions:

1. **What is the question or claim?**
2. **What public source can another person inspect?** Include an official URL, DOI or dataset accession/version.
3. **What did you observe, and how can it be reproduced?** Separate the original source's statements from your interpretation.
4. **What does this *not* prove?** State important uncertainty, contradictory findings or data limitations.

We distinguish:
- **Source-verified observation:** What an accessible public document/dataset directly shows.
- **Interpretation:** A plausible meaning of that observation, subject to assumptions.
- **Hypothesis:** An idea to test, not an established result.
- **Clinical evidence:** Must be interpreted within the applicable trial, population, disease and safety constraints.

A public contribution is **not automatically accepted science**. Editors may request revisions, correction, verification, expert assessment or refuse unsuitable material. Acceptance into a curated directory means metadata/source quality checked, **not** clinical validity.

## Lightweight review workflow

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Submitted | Public form received; not verified |
| Safety screening | Remove/reject private or improperly shared content; do not reproduce it in replies |
| Source check | Official URL exists, facts and attribution checked |
| Reproducibility check | Another reviewer can follow the steps where applicable |
| Accepted, amended or closed | Credited with consent; transparent corrections and review history |

Participation by children or novices should focus on safe public research information, not personal medical stories or live patient care. Scientific and clinical questions may need domain-expert review.

## Accessibility, citations and credit

Write in plain language. Explain each technical term before using abbreviations. Prefer short, verifiable statements and original sources. Credit others accurately; only contribute work you can lawfully share. Public Issues expose the submitting GitHub username. Accepted content may link to the original issue; if attribution or licensing is uncertain, clarify it **before** merging.

## Longer-term vision

We may later offer separate, protected, opt-in case or data contributions, but only after defining ethics/legal review, explicit informed consent, data minimization, de-identification and re-identification testing, secure access and retention, appropriate governance, and protections for minors. Nothing here authorizes collection of such material.
