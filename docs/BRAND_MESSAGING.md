# Brand and homepage messaging principles

Updated: October 27, 2026.

## Positioning

**Visitor benefit first.** A curious visitor may never have heard of NAMs, proteomics, immunoinformatics, or virtual laboratories. Lead with their problem: promising questions, scattered resources, opaque access terms, and uncertainty about where to start.

**Core promise:** Make it easier to discover credible research resources, compare access conditions, and identify a practical starting point. Do **not** promise faster cures, personal medical advice, independently verified experiments, or that every tool is free.

## Homepage narrative

1. **Human problem:** “You have a question worth investigating.”
2. **Friction:** Research tools are scattered; users encounter unfamiliar terms and ambiguous costs.
3. **Opportunity:** Public resources and guided pathways are brought into one directory, with links and caveats.
4. **Next step:** Visitor finds a source, evaluates whether it is appropriate, and moves toward a testable question.
5. **Evidence boundaries:** Simulated or predicted results still require replication, specialist oversight when relevant, and biological validation.

### Current hero messaging

**Headline:** Less time searching. More time exploring what matters.

**Supporting statement:** You have a question worth investigating. But useful tools are scattered, costs are unclear, and it's hard to know where to start. Find credible research resources together, see what's really free, and take your next step with greater confidence.

**Primary action:** Find the right resources → directory.

**Secondary action:** Help me get started → guided research paths.

**Illustrated example:** A practical 3-step journey: the question → find a starting point → move forward with evidence. This is explicitly illustrative, not a promise of clinical effectiveness.

## Where features belong

Under the first viewport, explain search, filters, resource count, simulation tools, datasets, NAMs, APIs, and community contributions. Features should support a human-outcome narrative, never replace it.

### Personal founder association

**“Healing at the Pace of AI”** is a personal mission/brand association and may appear modestly in About, founder context, or editorial materials. It is not the primary site headline, the official name of the directory, or a claim that AI improves clinical outcomes.

The resource center remains independent and open to general biomedical discovery. Avoid implying any individual medical case is represented, or that a commercial service supports the project.

## Editorial voice

- Compassionate without exploiting urgency, illness, or emotion.
- Plain language before scientific jargon.
- Benefits before features, and actionable links over hype.
- Explain costs and access clearly. Never suggest a resource is free just because its website is public.
- Separate research hypotheses, computational predictions, scientific findings, and clinical evidence.
- No unearned outcome promises or invented endorsements.

## Future testing

Compare headline comprehension with newcomers and researchers, including mobile visitors. Ask: “What does this site help you do?” A visitor should be able to answer after seeing only the first screen.

Measure legitimate discovery events (directory visits, useful official-link clicks) only with privacy-preserving, optional analytics. No tracking is currently installed.
