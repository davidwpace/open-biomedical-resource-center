## What problem is addressed?

A T-cell receptor (TCR) on a T cell may recognize a short peptide displayed by a human leukocyte antigen (HLA) molecule. Predicting which combinations bind can help prioritize *research hypotheses* about immune recognition, vaccine antigens and engineered T-cell receptors.

Previous models often used only short TCR sequence fragments, and negative examples may be generated by randomly pairing receptors and peptides without strong experimental evidence of nonbinding. Dataset construction and model evaluation are therefore vulnerable to confounding and artificial shortcuts.

Hi-TPH brings together four increasingly informative feature levels and a designed approach to selecting negatives during training. The paper reports **19.2% relative benchmark improvement over comparative approaches on an external wet-lab test set**. This is an **author-reported evaluation result**, not a fixed improvement in every dataset and not a clinical response rate. Earlier 2025 preprint material reported **17.4%**, so use the October 2026 peer-reviewed version for the current 19.2% claim.

| III | Peptide + HLA + TCR CDR3α and CDR3β |
| IV | Peptide + HLA + full TCR α and β variable-region sequences |

The model repository hosts protein language model checkpoints across multiple architecture sizes. Small variants may be more practical for local reproduction, but any run must be benchmarked for your own hardware, dependencies and held-out dataset. Do **not** assume the 19.2% improvement applies to one selected small checkpoint.

## Scope and limitations

- Classification/recognition models may not predict immunogenicity, tumor antigen processing, immune-cell activation, tissue infiltration, safety, or tumor response.
- Synthetic or on-the-fly negative sampling is intended to reduce biases; it does not guarantee that all nonbinding labels are biologically proven.
- Strong validation requires independent, preferably prospective experimental datasets; negative controls, group-aware data splits, peptide/TCR leakage checks, HLA-distribution checks, and uncertainty analyses.
- Full paired TCR sequences and accurate HLA typing are not available in every dataset or individual clinical case.
- **Noncommercial license:** the authors state the dataset and archive are CC BY-NC 4.0. GitHub's repository LICENSE also displays CC BY-NC 4.0. Do not copy, bundle, monetize or relicense the authors' code/data/checkpoints without confirming relevant terms. This directory links externally.
- Computational execution can require significant GPU memory, disk space, compatible Python/PyTorch versions, and scientific software experience. “Free to download” does not mean “free to compute”.
- No patient-identifying or privately sourced sequencing data belong in this public repository.

## Responsible verification checklist

1. Inspect GitHub README, current commit, published supplemental methods, and the archive version; document changes and dataset provenance.
2. Choose a realistic subset or checkpoint first; record exact code/model versions and environment.
3. Reproduce a published **held-out** benchmark and check data split strategy before accepting performance numbers.
4. Compare a simple baseline and several published predictors using consistent metrics; report confidence intervals and class balance.
5. Test generalization to unseen peptides, TCRs, HLA alleles and laboratory sources; inspect possible overlaps with training data.
6. Assess calibration, false-positive rates and selective prediction; do not use a single average metric to claim clinical accuracy.
7. Clearly separate **research predictions** from experimental validation and clinical treatment recommendations.
