THE MYSTERY · 1/4

Why did losing the pancreas cause diabetes?

Researchers had linked the pancreas to blood sugar control, but the key internal substance was hard to isolate and use.

Here's the lesson: A useful question often starts with an observation and something still missing.

THE EARLY EXPERIMENTS · 2/4

Banting and Best tested a possibility.

In 1921, Frederick Banting and Charles Best, working with laboratory resources arranged by J. J. R. Macleod, tested pancreatic extracts in diabetic animals. The results were promising.

Here's the lesson: Promising animal findings aren't proof that a treatment will work in people.

See supporting source ↗

THE IMPROVEMENT · 3/4

Cleaner extracts changed the outcome.

Biochemist James Collip improved purification. In January 1922, a more purified preparation produced a clear benefit for Leonard Thompson, an early patient. The first preparation had been less successful.

Here's the lesson: Research often advances by improving and repeating an imperfect first attempt.

See supporting source ↗

THE LESSON · 4/4

Breakthroughs need different skills.

Experimental design, laboratory work, chemical purification, clinical care and later large-scale production all contributed. The story is about collaboration, not a single magical moment.

Here's the lesson: A contribution can be a better method, a verification, or a key improvement.
